# Craton: product strategy (short version)

## The problem
Geology is three-dimensional and largely invisible, but students learn it from flat diagrams, and many African universities and schools have few lab resources, limited field trips, weak internet and budget phones.

## Positioning
**Africa's 3D geology course.**
- The whole physical geology course in one app, not a single tool.
- Predict-first active learning, with exam-style practice and a daily habit.
- Works offline on any phone, with zero asset downloads.
- Grounded in Nigerian and African geology, starting with Nigeria and then regional packs.

## Lessons from Elsevier's Complete Anatomy
Elsevier bought 3D4Medical (Complete Anatomy) in November 2019 for about US$50 million. It works because the subject is compulsory and hard, lecturers build it into teaching (so students must use it), and professionals keep paying after graduation. Craton mirrors this with:
- exam-focused features, so students need it
- lecturer tools, so classes are assigned to it
- Craton Pro for real borehole and drillhole data, so graduates keep using it

## Competition
- **Visible Geology (Seequent, free since 2024):** structural tools (block builder, stereonet, apparent dip). It is strong and free, so do not compete head-on on structural tools.
- **University tools** (GeoBlocks 3D, AR apps, virtual field trips) and **rock-ID apps** each cover one slice.
- **The gap:** a whole-course, predict-first, offline, Africa-grounded 3D atlas.

## Revenue paths
1. **Free core** for every student: the Atlas, the daily challenge and the Nigeria map.
2. **Student Pro** (per semester): full exam bank, all puzzles, sync.
3. **School or department licence** (yearly): Pro for everyone, plus class tools.
4. **Corporate sponsorship:** energy, mining and water companies sponsor cohorts or host-community schools; credited in the app, with a yearly impact report.
5. **Later: Craton Pro for industry:** borehole and drillhole data turned into 3D for drillers, geotechnical firms and small miners.

## Next validation steps
- Pilot with 2–3 geology departments and 1–2 secondary schools, and measure test scores before and after.
- Hold five conversations with borehole drillers or geotechnical engineers about Craton Pro.
- Get a geology lecturer to review all content.
- Check the name "Craton" is available as a business name, domain and trademark before printing anything.
